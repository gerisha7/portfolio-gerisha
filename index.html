06 / BRAND NOTES

Campaigns I can't stop thinking about.

Four pieces of work I'd happily defend in an interview. For each: the insight, the move, why it worked, and the one thing I'd steal.

my take, not the press release
The insight

People forget ads. They remember conversations. A brand that shows up inside today's conversation gets remembered with it.

The move

Since March 1966, the Amul girl has commented on news, cricket, films and politics, often within days of the event. The work came from Sylvester daCunha's agency, ASP, with illustrator Eustace Fernandes.

Why it worked

The format does the brand building and the topic does the attention grabbing. Same girl, same tagline, a new headline every time, for about sixty years.

What I'd steal

Build a format, not a campaign. A repeatable format lets a brand be topical without reinventing itself every week.

Source: Storyboard18 ↗
The insight

After the pandemic, neighbourhood retailers were struggling through Diwali, and those same stores are where Cadbury actually gets sold.

The move

AI recreated Shah Rukh Khan's face and voice so any local store owner could generate an ad of SRK naming their shop. 85,000+ retailers were featured across 139,000 ad versions.

Why it worked

It turned celebrity reach into a gift for the brand's own channel partners. The purpose and the business goal were the same thing. Reported 22% higher view-through and 18% higher click-through rates, and India's first Titanium Lion at Cannes (2022).

What I'd steal

Look at who stands between the brand and the buyer. Sometimes the smartest audience is the channel, not the consumer.

Source: Adgully ↗
The insight

Dove's research found only 4% of women describe themselves as beautiful. The problem wasn't beauty. It was self-perception.

The move

A forensic sketch artist drew each woman twice, once from her own description and once from a stranger's, then showed her both portraits side by side.

Why it worked

It didn't tell women they were beautiful. It showed them evidence. The insight became the demo. It passed 163 million views by June 2013 and won the Titanium Grand Prix at Cannes.

What I'd steal

Don't argue with a belief. Design an experiment that lets people disprove it themselves.

Source: Wikipedia ↗
The insight

Everyone knows Rahul Dravid as "The Wall": composed, polite, predictable. Surprise needs a strong expectation to break.

The move

Released on 9 April 2021 during the IPL, the ad cast Dravid as a road-raging "gunda" of Indiranagar, yelling and smashing in traffic. It was written by a team that included Tanmay Bhat and Devaiah Bopanna.

Why it worked

The bigger the gap between the reputation and the role, the harder people stop scrolling. It went viral within hours.

But

Plenty of viewers asked where CRED was in its own ad. Attention is not the same as understanding what the brand does.

What I'd steal

Break an expectation people already hold, but make the brand the reason for the surprise, not a footnote.
